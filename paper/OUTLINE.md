# Manuscript outline

## Working title

**Leakage-controlled behaviour coding and disagreement modelling in AnnoMI**

## Central argument

Evaluation boundaries and target definitions materially change what can be learned from AnnoMI.
Under source-held-out evaluation, a target-only pretrained encoder provides the clearest supported
classification gain, whereas additional dialogue context and task coupling do not provide supported
improvements. Existing multi-annotator labels also permit prediction of graded coding disagreement.
The Task A/C extensions expose a trade-off between discrimination, proper scoring, and useful set
size, but their strongest values remain exploratory or post-hoc.

## 1. Introduction

- Motivate automatic research coding of motivational-interviewing demonstrations while separating
  it from clinical decision support.
- Explain why transcript-level splitting can be insufficient when multiple transcripts originate
  from one video source.
- Frame annotation disagreement as part of the target rather than only as label noise.
- State three contributions:
  1. a source-disjoint, nested evaluation of sparse and pretrained-text baselines;
  2. an exhaustive seven-transcript study of empirical vote-distribution prediction;
  3. a transparent Task A/C negative-transfer study with calibrated action sets and a pre-access
     external-overlap audit.
- Do not assert methodological priority or state-of-the-art performance.

## 4. Study I: source-disjoint therapist-behaviour coding

### 4.1 Models and evaluation

Compare target-utterance TF–IDF, target-only RoBERTa, causal-history RoBERTa, and the gated
target/history architecture. Report source-balanced macro-F1 as primary, with Brier score and log
loss as probability-quality measures. Make clear that all five outer folds are included.

### 4.2 Results

- Supported result: target-only RoBERTa improves macro-F1 from 0.7172 to 0.8108; paired difference
  0.0935, 95% source-bootstrap interval [0.0778, 0.1097].
- Numerical-only result: causal-history RoBERTa reaches 0.8163, but its F1 interval versus target
  only crosses zero and its log loss is worse.
- Adaptive negative result: the gated context architecture reaches 0.8116 and neither its model
  contrast nor its matched context ablation supports an improvement.

## 5. Study II: predicting coding disagreement

### 5.1 Models and evaluation

Describe leave-one-transcript-out evaluation, transcript-balanced weighting, full vote
distributions, and the distinction between therapist and client label spaces. Compare a transcript
prior, plurality-trained linear model, soft-distribution linear model, and a centered low-rank
annotator-conditioned model. The inferential unit is the transcript, not the individual annotation.

### 5.2 Results

- The soft-distribution model improves vote log score over the transcript prior for therapist and
  client turns, with intervals excluding zero in the favorable direction.
- The annotator-conditioned model fails its primary therapist log-score gate. Its better
  Jensen–Shannon divergence and entropy error are secondary trade-offs, not grounds to reverse the
  primary conclusion.
- Soft versus hard-label training is unresolved in this seven-transcript sample.

## 6. Study III: early quality and next recorded action

### 6.1 Commit-locked joint study

Define Task A as prediction of uploader-designated demonstration quality from an absolute causal
prefix and Task C as prediction of the next recorded therapist behaviour before its text is seen.
Describe uncertainty-propagating predicted histories, the recurrent encoder, latent quality mixture,
fit-source transition estimates, and held-out-source calibration.

Report Q-TRACE-MI's Task A point estimate of 0.7000 balanced accuracy at ten therapist turns, while
retaining the interval [-0.0734, 0.1731] for its +0.0500 contrast with A-only. For Task C, report
that Q-TRACE-MI is worse than C-only by -0.0509 macro-F1, interval [-0.0768, -0.0258]. The joint
gate fails.

### 6.2 Exploratory asymmetric-transfer study

Describe the staged comparison of frozen and fold-adapted representations, detached one-way
transfer, discounted evidence, bounded transition residuals, and source-safe prototype retrieval.
Separate the commit-locked exploratory v2 screen from the v2.1 post-hoc extension.

- Task A: the post-hoc one-way model reaches 0.7389 balanced accuracy, but its interval against
  A-only crosses zero and Brier score worsens.
- Task C: the matched frozen-GRU baseline reaches 0.4359 macro-F1. No tested candidate exceeds it;
  prototype retrieval has a lower Brier point estimate without a supported F1 gain.
- Outer-cross-fitted adapted-GRU sets reach 0.8072 coverage with mean size 2.4845. These are
  descriptive four-class action sets, not intervention recommendations.

## 7. External evaluation boundary

Explain that the MI-TAGS protocol was committed before public-sample retrieval. The sample audit
found 12 records, quarantined 9 possible overlaps, and left only two records in the locked test
partition before quarantine, below the required 20 groups. Report no external performance metric.
Authorized access to the full corpus and the unchanged overlap/split procedure are prerequisites to
an external-generalization analysis.

## 8. Discussion

- Interpret the supported target-only encoder result as evidence for source-held-out research
  coding, not as a claim that context is never useful.
- Discuss why present-utterance signal, small cluster counts, and probability-quality trade-offs may
  explain the limited value of added context and coupling.
- Treat predictable vote distributions as evidence that ambiguity is structured within this panel,
  while avoiding population-wide claims about annotators.
- Present the retained failures as evidence about model complexity and negative transfer rather
  than selecting whichever secondary metric is favorable.

## 9. Limitations and responsible use

Cover demonstration-versus-care distribution shift, the seven-transcript disagreement sample,
uploader metadata as Task A target, absence of demographic attributes, dependence on transcription
and annotation conventions, post-selection in SAFE-MI, possible cross-corpus source reuse, and the
absence of any clinical-outcome evaluation. Explicitly rule out diagnosis, therapist ranking,
treatment recommendation, and autonomous use.
